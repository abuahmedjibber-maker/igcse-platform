Language focus: relative clauses

A relative clause gives more information about a person, thing, place or time. A non-defining relative clause adds extra information, so we put it between commas. If you remove it, the sentence still makes sense.

The World Scout Jamboree, which is held every four years, is designed for young people aged 14 to 17.

!
Remember: always use a comma before who or which in a non-defining clause, and never use that in a non-defining clause. Do not repeat the pronoun: Omar, whose mother is a doctor, — not whose his mother.
